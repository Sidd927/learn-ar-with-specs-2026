## Script: read exactly, in this order
Don't explain, hint, point, or use the words *mistake, correct, wrong, better, AR*. If they're stuck for more than 30 s, use the **fallback prompt** and tick "prompted".

| # | Say | Fallback prompt (only if stuck > 30 s) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Please try this however you like. You can think aloud. I won't explain it yet." *(Watch for up to 2 minutes.)* | "Is there anything on the screen you haven't tried yet?" Then, if needed: "There are buttons called Knees, Heels and Chest." |
| 2 | "In your own words, what do you think this is meant to teach?" | — |
| 3 | For each of Knees, Chest, Heels they open: "What, if anything, is different between the two figures?" | If one isn't opened: "Could you try [Knees / Chest / Heels]?" |
| 4 | "The view changed when you pressed that. Why do you think it changed?" | — |
| 5 | *(Observe only.)* Do they get back to the normal demonstration on their own? | "How would you get back to where you were?" |
| 6 | *(Reset the preview, or look away from the screen.)* "What, if anything, would you remember from this?" | — |
| 7 | "What was the most confusing moment?" | — |
| 8 | "If you could change one thing, what would it be?" | — |

**What counts as identified** (they don't need these words):
- **Knees:** the red knees are closer together, or inward, or not over the feet.
- **Chest:** the red body leans or tips further forward, or the chest is lower.
- **Heels:** the red heels are off the floor, or up on the toes.

Use **Partly** for an answer about the right body area with the wrong change (for heels, for example "the red legs move").
